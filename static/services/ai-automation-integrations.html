System integration

Three ways to connect systems, and how we choose

Not every connection needs custom code, and not every one should be a quick no-code recipe. We pick the simplest approach that is reliable for your case.

Native integration

Built-in connectors

Use the integration that already exists between two platforms, configured and mapped properly.

Best when: The connector covers the fields and behavior you need and your plan supports it.

No-code / low-code

Workflow automation

Build workflows in a tool such as n8n that call each platform and transform data between them.

Best when: You need flexible logic, several systems in one flow, or something a native connector cannot do.

Custom API development

Code-level integration

Write and host custom code against platform APIs, with authentication, queues, logging and tests.

Best when: Volume, security, complex mapping or business rules go beyond what a workflow tool should carry.

We confirm before we promise. We only commit to a specific integration after checking that the platforms expose the APIs or objects you need, what access and permissions are required, and what limits apply. If something is not feasible, we tell you early and suggest an alternative.

FAQs

Questions we often hear

Do you use AI everywhere?

No. We use AI where it helps, such as summarizing, classifying, extracting and drafting, and keep ordinary rule-based automation for anything that must be exact and predictable. Important actions keep a human approval step.

Which integrations can you build?

We cannot responsibly list every combination up front. We confirm feasibility, API access, permissions and limits for your specific systems during discovery, then commit to what is achievable.

What is n8n?

n8n is a workflow automation platform that connects apps and services through visual workflows. It can be self-hosted or used as a cloud service, which makes it a good fit when you want more control than typical no-code tools.

How do you handle data privacy with AI tools?

We review which data would be sent to which service, apply the minimum necessary, and keep sensitive information out of third-party AI tools unless you have approved that and the provider terms support it. We cannot guarantee a third party's behavior, so we document what goes where.

What happens when an automation fails?

Workflows include error handling and alerts so failures are visible. Where possible they retry safely, and the runbook explains how to investigate and recover.

Can you take over automations someone else built?

Yes. We review what exists, document it, fix what is fragile and decide with you what to keep.

Which task should stop eating your team's time?

Describe the process and the tools involved. We will tell you what can be automated and what should stay manual.
